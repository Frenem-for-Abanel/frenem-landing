A structure that lives in a deck is already out of date. Someone drew it for
a meeting, the room nodded, and then the organisation kept moving. By the
next quarter the slide is a souvenir: still presented, no longer true.

The interesting problem is not drawing boxes. It is refusing a second picture
of the organisation, one that can drift from the one people actually work in.
Prism treats the chart as the record, not as an illustration of a record kept
somewhere else. If the picture and the reporting line disagree, the picture
is wrong.

That is a placement decision. Illustration is cheap and it ages in a drawer.
A single picture, the one the work runs on, is the only one that can stay
honest.
